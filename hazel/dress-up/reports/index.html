Dress Up reports

Reports on Messed Up Dress Up, the game made with M and N, now a hybrid: JavaScript, with Hazel as the scripting language for its game logic (by default; Fumola beside it for comparison), where features are tried with real players before they are rebuilt as livelits in Hazel. Each report takes one question and answers it with measurements. The first rule they measure: 60 frames a second for everything a game controller does. Matthew Hammer.

The reports themselves live with the game, in its repository; Hazel's own series is the Hazel reports.

    60 frames a second, and what checks it

    Does the game hold 60 frames a second for what a game controller does in a place, how much of that do our tests check, and where do they run? Every measured case holds 59.3 to 60 on 0.0.78: walking and dragging the friend in seven places, at 1920×1080. What made the difference was in the browser's work, not the game's: masks, a repeated write, blurs, the imagination's live pictures. The measuring covers 2 of 8 kinds of action in 7 of 24 places, and none of it runs in CI yet.
