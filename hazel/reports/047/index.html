First-Order Data in a Form of Its Own

A design note for dev. Closed, first-order values (numbers, strings, booleans, lists, tuples, constructors) pay for the generality of open terms wherever they go: ids on every node, environments substituted into them, a hash of every node to share them. Matthew, 2026-11-08: "let's try to do something systematic for dev that solves this, at the cost of another representation form for first-order data, and no closures", and then: "I'm imagining a special case of Value (or whatever it is called in the core language) def that's especially efficient to serialize for first-order data." And: "Fumola is more efficient than Hazel for these values, and we should try to experiment with these other, more efficient design ideas and submit a plan to the group based on what we find." This note proposes that value form, how it is made and sent, and the experiments to run first; the plan for the group comes from what they find.

Outline#

  1. The goal: a 60 fps floor for small edits; the Witch slide is at 1.3 to 1.7 s an edit.
  2. Why: the same cost, found again and again; last on the Witch slide, where one module written out in a probe's environment was 3.8 MB of a 5.4 MB answer.
  3. The form: a Data term, a value made only by the evaluator, with no ids, holes, closures or environments inside it.
  4. Beside Fumola's Value: Fumola keeps values apart from terms, and its first-order cases serialize as they are; the cases here are those, inside Hazel's terms.
  5. Why it is cheap to send: no ids, no sharing to find, numbers packed; or not encoded at all, as plain JS values over the browser's own copy.
  6. Which code made a value: kept beside it, keyed by the code's id, as probe samples already are, and as Fumola's graph keeps it; never in the value.
  7. When a value becomes data: first where values leave the evaluator, then in the evaluator itself.
  8. Where it pays, in order: probe samples, the worker's messages, livelit models, hazel-jit, the evaluator.
  9. What it costs: a second form wherever values are read, and the same answers both ways.
  10. What it is not: no sharing, no closures, no holes, no types inside.
  11. The experiments: the same values as Hazel's terms, as data, and as Fumola's values, sent, built and read; measured before anything is proposed.
  12. Experiment 1: sending the Witch's picture: as data, 3.0 times smaller and 2.5 to 3.9 times quicker to send than as terms; as JS values or JSON, quicker still once made; Fumola's side beside it; and treemaps of where the bytes are.
  13. The plan for the group: written from the experiments; a draft of its slices, and questions for Matthew.

The goal: 60 fps for a small edit#

A slider's step drawn in 16.7 ms, a 60 fps floor for small edits, as a JS app holds; the Witch slide is at 1.3 to 1.7 s, about 80 to 100 times that. Matthew, 2026-11-08: "I'd really like Hazel to have a floor of 60FPS for small edits (sliders changing), just like in JS (but well beyond there, obviously)." And: "And each obstacle is worth studying, and overcoming." Where the Witch's small edit stands (^report(46), two cuts, and sharing dropped): the worker's evaluator 0.81 to 0.92 s; the page's own work, about 0.4 to 0.8 s, most of it the whole slide's view drawn again, about 117 ms each time and five times an edit; the trip between the worker and the page, 79 to 102 ms. First-order data is one obstacle among these: it is in what the evaluator builds and in what the worker sends.

Why#

Hazel holds every value as a term, and a closed, first-order value pays for what a term can be: open, holed, a closure over an environment. The cost has come up again and again:

  • Probe samples. A sample keeps, beside its value, the bindings its expression names, each value with the environment substituted into it (Sample.Env.elide). On the Witch slide, the ⇓ toggle's probe on the program's last line, a closed, first-order tuple of a few bytes, kept the Drawing module that way: 3.8 MB of the worker's 5.4 MB answer (^report(46), what the answer holds). With function-holding values left out, the answer was 0.42 MB, most of it the picture written out again in four samples, each copy fresh (two cuts).
  • The trip between the worker and the page. Encoding a term with every node's ids, then a hash of every node to share what the page held, then decoding it: 70% of a small edit on that slide (the trip), and the sharing cost more than it saved once the answer was small; it is gone from the answers (Matthew: "drop sharing").
  • hazel-jit. A compiled view's model goes into JS, and its answer came back as terms: the way back was most of a compiled render, and making ids for it a large part (^report(45)).
  • Before that. ^ticket(666), how much sharing is lost passing data to and from the worker; ^ticket(681), JS values converted directly at the call boundary; ^ticket(710), lists as trees; ^ticket(513), the sharing itself; and the note in CachedStatics.compute_targets, where samples' environment copies were once about 80% of retained memory.

Dev pays more than the branches measured there. Its worker's answer is the run's value and final state as they are: not pruned, with the observation trace, and with the whole incremental cache, which goes to the page and comes back with the next request (WorkerServer.Request.value.prev).

The form#

A new value form in the core language: Data, a closed, first-order value with nothing inside it but data. Dev has no type of values apart from terms: a value is a term that ValueChecker calls a Value. So the special case is a term:

/* Grammar.exp_term, beside Atom, ListLit, Tuple, Constructor, ... */
| Data(Data.t)

/* Data.re */
type t =
  | Atom(Atom.t)                  /* Int, SInt, Nat, Float, Bool, String: the term's own atoms */
  | List(list(t))
  | Tuple(list(field))            /* labeled or not; () is Tuple([]) */
  | Ctor(string, option(t))       /* a constructor, alone or applied */
and field = {
  label: option(string),
  value: t,
};
  • No source ids. A value has none: not below the Data node, and not on it, whose tag is one shared, empty tag, not a fresh id per value. Which code made a value is kept beside it (below). (Matthew, 2026-11-08: "IMO -- Values should not have source ids. That makes very little sense. That association should be stored elsewhere --- like a global DCG, in the case of Fumola.")
  • Nothing inside but data. No holes, no closures, no environments, no ascriptions. So it is closed: substitution passes over it without a walk, and a probe sample keeps it as it is.
  • Made only by the evaluator. The user's syntax has no way to write it, and the statics never see it in a program; only values are ever data.
  • Types. A term's constructor carries its type for the statics of results (Constructor(string, option(option(typ)))). Data does not, below its root: the Data node can carry the value's type once, where the evaluator knows it, and turning data back into a term reads the constructors' types from it.
  • Big numbers. Atom.t as dev has it: Int and Nat are big integers, SInt a machine one.

Beside Fumola's Value#

Fumola keeps its values apart from its terms, and each first-order case of its Value is plain data that serializes and deserializes as it is. The cases proposed here are those cases, inside Hazel's terms. (Matthew, 2026-11-08: "For comparison --- Fumola has a Value type. Each first-order case of Value (most of them) is simple to serialize and deserialize.")

  • Fumola's Value (crates/fumola_semantics/src/lib/value.rs, at cb2bcf5): Null, Bool, Unit, Nat and Int (big), Float, Char, Text, Blob, Array, Tuple, Object, Option, Variant (a name and an optional value); then the cases that are not data: pointers, closed functions, primitive functions, modules, quoted code, actors, Adapton's pointers, times, spaces and histories, thunks. Every case derives serde's Serialize and Deserialize; the first-order ones need nothing more, and values come in from JSON as they are (fumola_define_json, #148 in the Fumola repo).
  • No ids in a value. The program's ids are in its terms, and a value is not a term, so a value carries none: the cost Hazel's terms pay at every node is not there to pay. Which code made a value is in the run's graph (below).
  • No sharing written. Arrays and tuples are persistent vectors (im-rc), shared by reference in memory and cheap to grow and index; serde writes a shared value where it is met, as a tree. That is what Matthew expects of data here.
  • What Hazel's form takes from it. The same shape: Data's Atom, List, Tuple and Ctor are Fumola's atoms, Array, Tuple and Object, and Variant; no ids; each case encoded as it is; and the same line between data and the rest, closures on the other side.
  • What differs. In Hazel a value is a term, so that a result can hold holes and be stepped and printed as code. So Data is a case of the term, made by the evaluator, not a separate type the evaluator returns. A separate value type, as Fumola has, every result a value and terms kept for holes and the stepper, is the larger alternative; it would change the evaluator and the stepper throughout, and is not proposed here.

Why it is cheap to send#

Three things a term costs to encode that data does not: its ids, a search for sharing, and boxed numbers. And data could skip encoding altogether.

  • No ids. Every term node carries an id tag (IdTagged.IdTag.t): its ids, each a 16-byte UUID, and the secondary runs, incomplete tiles and lexeme the editor keeps for a written term, empty for one the evaluator made, but there. A list of 1,000 numbers as terms is about 1,000 cons blocks, 1,000 term records, 1,000 id tags with their id lists and UUIDs; as data, 1,000 cons blocks and the numbers.
  • No sharing to find. Marshal keeps a table of every block it has written, so that a block met twice is written once; for terms with environments that is needed. Data is not expected to share (Matthew, 2026-11-08: "in first order data --- we don't expect any"), so it can be written as a tree, with no table: Marshal's No_sharing, or an encoder of its own.
  • Numbers packed. An encoder of its own can write a list of floats as one array of floats: the Witch's 924 points are 1,848 numbers, about 15 KB as doubles, against about 0.13 MB for the picture as terms (an estimate from ^report(46)'s readings, not measured).
  • Or not encoded at all. Data turned into plain JS values (arrays, numbers, strings) crosses postMessage by the browser's own structured copy, in native code, with no Marshal in js_of_ocaml on either side. The browser's copy recurses: it overflowed on deep terms (#2368), so a long list would cross as an array, its depth that of the data's nesting, not its length. A message would then be its terms, encoded as now, and beside them the data, as JS values.

Which of these to build first is a measurement: the first slice times each on the answers it changes.

Which code made a value: kept beside it#

A value carries no source ids; the association between a value and the code that made it is kept elsewhere, keyed by the code's id. Hazel already keeps most of it that way.

  • Already beside the value. A probe's samples are keyed by the probed expression's id (Sample.Map: a syntax id to its samples); a test's reports by the test's id (TestMap); the observation trace names the code of each span it opens and closes; a livelit's model is found by the model's id in the samples.
  • In Fumola, a global graph. In Fumola's graphical mode, a run's demanded computation graph names each node by a symbol and a time and records its thunk and the value it holds; asking which code made a value is a question for the graph, not for the value.
  • What reads a value's ids on dev today is slice 1's first list: each reader moves to an association kept beside the value, or reads a term made from the data when it needs one. A value the evaluator makes gets fresh ids today, which name no code, so most such readers should be few.

When a value becomes data#

First where a value leaves the evaluator; then in the evaluator itself.

  • At the edges. A check, Data.of_term : Exp.t => option(Data.t), walks a value and gives up at the first function, closure, hole or open term; Data.to_term makes the term back, with fresh ids, only when something needs a term (a probe's drawer prints a term today). Where the statics know the value's type, a first-order type (no arrows, nothing unknown: Typ.has_fun_up_to_aliases and its like) says so before the walk.
  • In the evaluator. A tuple, list or constructor whose parts are all data is made as data, so values stay data as they are built, and are never turned into data at the edges. Then pattern matching, the builtins on lists and numbers, equality and printing read data directly: a match of hd :: tl against Data(List([a, ...rest])) binds hd to a and tl to Data(List(rest)). This is the larger change, and the one that keeps values from ever being terms.

Where it pays, in order#

  1. Probe samples (dev). A sample's value, and each binding in its environment, is data, or opaque (a function, or a value holding one), or, only when it is open, a term. The drawer turns data into a term when it draws it, once per sample shown. This fixes the Witch's case at its root, and every probe on dev with it.
  2. The worker's messages (dev). The program's value, the samples, and the incremental cache's states, in both directions, carry data; measured as bytes and as encode and decode time on each side.
  3. Livelit models (the livelits branches, then dev when they land). A model is first-order by design, written into the program as a literal: data from the start, and each livelit's samples with it.
  4. hazel-jit (^report(45)). Data into JS and back, directly: arrays, numbers, strings, with no term made on the way back; ^ticket(681)'s idea, and the diffs of ^ticket(715) on data. ^ticket(710)'s lists as trees would be a change to Data.List, not to every term.
  5. The evaluator (above): values built as data.

What it costs#

  • A second form wherever values are read: the matcher, the builtins, equality (a data value against the same value as a term), printing, ValueChecker, the probes' drawers and their custom renderers (about ten places read sample.value in ProbeProj on dev). The edges come first because they need only of_term and to_term.
  • The same answers both ways. Tests: to_term(of_term(v)) equal to v up to ids for every value the test programs make; and every program's results, probes and tests the same with data on and off.
  • No sharing. A value held twice is written twice. Matthew does not expect data to share; a message that grows because of it would show in the measurements.
  • Floats. Atom.t compares floats by their printed form; data keeps that.

What it is not#

Not a change to holes or to open terms: anything that is not closed, first-order data stays a term, and behaves as it does now. No closures in data, and no holes; no sharing; no types below the root. Programs, and the editor's terms, are untouched: only values can be data.

The experiments#

Before a plan goes to the group, the same values measured each way: as Hazel's terms today, as the data form here, and as Fumola's values, which already work this way. Where things stand on the Witch slide (^report(46)): with a module kept out of a probe's environment, the trace cleared and the answers unshared, a small edit takes 1.3 to 1.7 s, and the worker's evaluator is 0.81 to 0.92 s of it, 50 to 64%; the trip is 5 to 8% (two cuts, and sharing dropped). So the evaluator, building values as terms, is now the larger cost, and sending them the smaller.

  1. Sending. The Witch's picture (133 shapes, 924 points, 046/data/witch/picture.json) and a probe-heavy answer, encoded and decoded: as Hazel's terms by Marshal (today); as data by Marshal with no sharing table; as data packed (numbers in float arrays); as plain JS values by the browser's structured copy; and as Fumola's Value through serde (its JSON door, and a binary form). Bytes, and ms each way, in Node and in Firefox.
  2. Building. The picture built by each evaluator: Hazel's today (terms, a fresh id per node); Hazel's with a prototype of the data form (values built as data); Fumola's. Time, and blocks allocated. ^report(29) already set the two evaluators side by side on eight small programs: Hazel took half Fumola's steps, and each cost about 20 times as much: 2.6 µs a pass against 0.13 µs a step. (Corrected 11-08, 08:33: this said about 15 times, from ^report(29)'s series card; its text says about 20.)
  3. Ids. How many fresh ids a run makes on the Witch slide, and what making them costs (^report(45) measured one id at a time; in ^report(29)'s profiles, fresh ids were 46% of one program's time); the first count of what reads a value's ids (above).
  4. Reading. A probe's drawer printing a sample: a term today; data turned into a term; data printed directly.
  5. Into JS. The picture into a compiled view: from Hazel's terms (hazel-jit's trip in, ^report(45)), from data, and from Fumola's values through fumola_wasm.

Where they run: Hazel's in Node on GitHub's machines (the test build is too heavy for the laptop) and in the Firefox check; Fumola's on the laptop, from its own repo (a cold build is about 3 minutes). Each result goes into this report as it comes, with its commit and run.

Experiment 1: sending the Witch's picture#

As data, the Witch's picture is 3.0 times smaller than as Hazel's terms (48.0 KB against 142.2 KB), and its trip by Marshal, encoded and decoded, takes 6.02 to 7.28 ms against 15.0 to 25.7, 2.5 to 3.9 times less. As plain JS values it would cross by the browser's own copy in 0.86 to 1.09 ms, once made JS (0.78 to 0.81 ms), or as JSON in 0.75 to 1.28 ms both ways. Fumola, natively, reads the same picture from JSON into its Value in 1.04 ms.

Times in Firefox are ranges over the check's runs (42493347395, 42494174912, 42494936942); Firefox's clock there ticks in whole milliseconds, and each time is the median of five batches of at least 25 ms.

  • What was measured. In the page, from the last answer the worker sent on the Witch slide after a slider's step: the picture, the probe sample with the most nodes as data (5,636), sent each way (DataExperiment, on trial/694-view-jit; 2432f0d166, run 42493347395, 42494174912, 42494936942). Every one of the answer's 108 samples converts to data (108): together 275.9 KB as terms, 144.7 KB as data.
  • No sharing table: larger, not faster. Data written with Marshal's No_sharing is larger, because a string met twice (a shape's colour, its kind) is then written twice; and it is hardly faster to write. Data is not expected to share, but its strings do, by accident of the evaluator; an encoder of its own could keep a table of strings alone.
  • Making data from a term. The prototype turns a value term into data after the fact, in 0.83 to 2.00 ms for the picture. (The first version took 110 ms: it stripped the ascriptions of the whole subterm at every node, so it was quadratic. Matthew: "can we fix the quadratic cost?" It peels each node's own now.) In the evaluator's form, a value would be made as data and never converted.
  • Fumola beside it (native, on the laptop, a release build; ^report(29) measured Fumola's wasm at 1.2 to 1.5 times its native time): the picture's JSON is 44.3 KB; read into a Value in 1.04 ms; written through Value's own serde derive in 0.27 ms and read back in 0.99 ms (that form tags every case, so it is wordier than the plain JSON). Hazel's numbers are Firefox's on GitHub's machines; the two are not the same machine, so read the comparison to within a factor of two or so.

Where the bytes are: treemaps#

The picture's bytes as Hazel's terms and as data, drawn as treemaps: each rectangle's area is its bytes, each shape an outer rectangle split into what its bytes are. Treemaps are Ben Shneiderman's, from the University of Maryland's Human-Computer Interaction Lab (1991), made first to show where a disk's space goes; the layout here is the squarified one of Bruls, Huijsing and van Wijk (2000), which keeps rectangles near square so their sizes compare by eye. (Matthew, 2026-11-08: "I'd really like to see 'tree maps' as figures (2d, not 1d) ... for before and after the first-order representation is applied.")

As Hazel's terms, the picture's ids and tags are 55.8 KB of its 142.2 KB (39%); the rest of the terms' structure 38.4 KB; the data's own structure 28.6 KB; and the data itself, its numbers and strings, 19.4 KB, 14% of the whole. As data, only the last two are left: 48.0 KB. Each amount is a difference of two exact sizes: the term against the same term with every id tag one shared tag; that against the data; the data against its numbers and strings alone.

The plan for the group#

Written from the experiments, for the group: which form, which edges first, and what each costs and saves, as measured. Until then, a draft of its slices:

  1. Data, of_term and to_term, with the round-trip tests and the list of what reads a value's ids on dev (above), and a Node benchmark on dev: a program whose probes name a module and a list of a thousand records, its worker answer's bytes and its encode and decode time, as terms and as data (each way of sending, above).
  2. Probe samples as data: the sample's value and environment; the drawer draws data. Measured with that benchmark and on dev's deploy in Firefox.
  3. The worker's messages: the value and the cache's states, and the way of sending the first slice found fastest.
  4. The Data term in the evaluator: values built as data; the matcher, the builtins, equality and printing.
  5. The livelits branches: models and livelit samples as data, and hazel-jit's trip; measured on the Witch's small edits (now 1.9 to 2.4 s, two cuts).

Questions for Matthew#

  1. The name: Data, or something else?
  2. Lists: an OCaml list, cheap to cons; an array, cheap to send and to index; a persistent vector, as Fumola's arrays are; or a level tree, as ^ticket(710) asks?
  3. Is the evaluator's form (slice 4) in scope from the start, or the edges first, as here? And is a separate value type, as Fumola has, worth weighing instead?
  4. Constructors' types: carried once at the root, as here, or not at all, the statics finding them again?
